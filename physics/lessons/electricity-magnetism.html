4.1Electricity & magnetism

Poles, fields and attraction

Like poles repel, unlike poles attract — and around every magnet is an invisible field you can map with a compass.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

4.1.1

Magnets and magnetic materials

  • a magnet has a north and a south pole; like poles repel, unlike poles attract
  • magnetic materials (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) are attracted by magnets; non-magnetic materials (copper, aluminium, wood, plastic) are not
  • a magnetic material can be magnetised (stroking with a magnet, or in a coil carrying d.c.) by induced magnetism
4.1.2

Magnetic fields

  • a magnetic field is a region where a magnetic pole experiences a force
  • the direction of the field at a point is the direction of the force on a north pole there; field lines go from N to S
  • plot field lines with a plotting compass or with iron filings; closer lines mean a stronger field
4.1.3

Temporary and permanent magnets

Magnetiseseasilyless easily
Keeps its magnetism?no — a temporary magnetyes — a permanent magnet
Used forelectromagnet cores, transformer corescompass needles, fridge magnets, loudspeakers

An electromagnet (a coil around a soft-iron core) can be switched on and off and its strength changed — used in scrapyard cranes, relays and electric bells.

4.2Electricity & magnetism

Charge, current, voltage, resistance

Current is the flow of charge; the potential difference pushes it; resistance opposes it. V = IR ties them together.

1

Understand

Five sections and a checklist

4.2.1

Electric charge

  • there are two kinds of charge, positive and negative; like charges repel, unlike attract
  • insulators can be charged by friction: electrons are rubbed from one material onto the other
  • conductors let charge (electrons) move freely; insulators don’t
  • Supp. an electric field is a region where a charge feels a force; field lines go from + to −; charge is measured in coulombs (C)
4.2.2

Electric current

  • current is the rate of flow of charge, measured in amperes with an ammeter in series
  • in metals the charge carriers are free electrons; conventional current flows from + to −, electrons flow the other way
  • d.c. flows one way only; a.c. keeps reversing direction

I = Q / t Supp.

4.2.3

E.m.f. and potential difference

  • e.m.f. is the electrical work done by a source in moving unit charge round a complete circuit (volts)
  • p.d. across a component is the work done per unit charge passing through it; measured with a voltmeter in parallel
4.2.4

Resistance

R = V / I

  • a longer wire has more resistance; a thicker wire less Supp.: R ∝ length, R ∝ 1/area
  • I–V graphs: a resistor at constant temperature gives a straight line; a filament lamp curves as it heats up; a diode conducts one way only
4.2.5

Electrical energy and power

P = I V · E = I V t · P = I² R

  • electrical energy is often measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh): a 1 kW appliance on for 1 hour
  • cost = energy used (kWh) × price per kWh

4.4Electricity & magnetism

Using electricity safely

Mains electricity can kill. Fuses, circuit breakers, earth wires and double insulation each stop a fault from becoming a shock or a fire.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

4.4.1

Hazards

  • damaged insulation — bare wires can give a shock
  • overheating of cables — too much current heats the wires and can start a fire
  • damp conditions — water conducts, increasing the risk of shock
  • overloading plugs and sockets — too many appliances draw too much current
4.4.2

Fuses and circuit breakers

  • a fuse is a thin wire in the live wire that melts if the current exceeds its rating, breaking the circuit
  • choose the fuse rating just above the normal current: I = P / V
  • a circuit breaker does the same job using an electromagnet that trips a switch; it can be reset, and acts faster
  • switches and fuses go in the live wire, so the appliance is disconnected from the high voltage
4.4.3

Earthing and double insulation

  • the earth wire connects a metal case to earth; if the live wire touches the case, a large current flows to earth and blows the fuse
  • double-insulated appliances have plastic cases (or no exposed metal), so they need no earth wire

4.5Electricity & magnetism

Moving magnets make current; current makes motion

Move a magnet near a wire and a voltage appears. Pass a current through a wire in a magnetic field and it feels a force. Generators, motors and transformers all rely on this.

1

Understand

Five sections and a checklist

4.5.1

Electromagnetic induction

  • moving a conductor through a magnetic field, or changing the field through a coil, induces an e.m.f.
  • the e.m.f. is bigger with faster movement, a stronger magnet and more turns on the coil
  • Supp. the induced e.m.f. causes a current whose magnetic field opposes the change causing it
4.5.2

The a.c. generator

  • a coil rotates in a magnetic field; the changing field through it induces an alternating e.m.f.
  • slip rings and brushes connect the rotating coil to the external circuit
  • Supp. the e.m.f. is greatest when the coil’s sides cut the field lines fastest (coil horizontal) and zero when it is vertical
4.5.3

Magnetic effect of a current

  • a current in a wire makes a circular magnetic field around it; in a solenoid the field is like a bar magnet’s
  • a bigger current, or more turns, makes a stronger field; reversing the current reverses it
  • uses: relays, electric bells, loudspeakers
4.5.4

Force on a current-carrying conductor; the d.c. motor

  • a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force; reversing the current or the field reverses the force (Fleming’s left-hand rule, Supp.)
  • a d.c. motor: forces on the two sides of a coil act in opposite directions, so the coil turns; a split-ring commutator reverses the current every half turn to keep it turning the same way
  • the motor turns faster with more current, more turns, or a stronger field
4.5.5

Transformers

Vp / Vs = Np / Ns · Supp. Vp Ip = Vs Is (100 % efficient)

  • an alternating current in the primary coil makes a changing field in the soft-iron core, which induces an alternating e.m.f. in the secondary coil
  • step-up transformers increase voltage (more secondary turns); step-down decrease it
  • electricity is transmitted at high voltage so the current is small, which reduces energy lost heating the cables (P = I²R)
