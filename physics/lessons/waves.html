3.1Waves, light & sound

Waves carry energy, not matter

Ripples, light and sound are all waves: a disturbance that moves energy from place to place. One equation links their speed, frequency and wavelength.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

3.1.1

Describing waves

TermMeaning
amplitudethe maximum displacement from the rest position
wavelength λthe distance between two neighbouring identical points (crest to crest)
frequency fthe number of waves passing a point each second (Hz)
wave speed vthe distance the wave moves each second
wavefronta line joining points on the same crest

v = f λ

3.1.2

Transverse and longitudinal waves

  • transverse: vibrations at right angles to the direction of travel — light and all electromagnetic waves, ripples on water, seismic S-waves
  • longitudinal: vibrations along the direction of travel, with compressions and rarefactions — sound, seismic P-waves
3.1.3

Reflection, refraction and diffraction

  • reflection at a barrier: angle of incidence = angle of reflection
  • refraction: a change of speed (e.g. water waves into shallower water) changes the wavelength and the direction
  • diffraction: waves spread out after passing through a gap or round an edge; most spreading when the gap is about the same size as the wavelength Supp.

3.2Waves, light & sound

Light reflects, bends and focuses

A mirror reflects at equal angles, glass slows light and bends it, and a curved lens bends every ray towards one point — the principal focus.

1

Understand

Five sections and a checklist

3.2.1

Reflection

  • angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal
  • an image in a plane mirror is virtual, upright, the same size, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, laterally inverted
3.2.2

Refraction

  • light changes speed when it passes into a different medium; entering a denser medium it slows and bends towards the normal
  • the refractive index n = sin i / sin r (light going from air into the material) Supp.; also n = speed in air ÷ speed in the material
3.2.3

Total internal reflection

  • when light goes from a denser to a less dense medium, at angles above the critical angle all of it is reflected
  • uses: optical fibres (communications, endoscopes), periscopes, bicycle reflectors

n = 1 / sin c Supp.

3.2.4

Thin lenses

  • a converging lens brings parallel rays together at the principal focus; the distance from the lens is the focal length
  • ray diagram: a ray parallel to the axis passes through F; a ray through the centre goes straight on
  • a real image is formed where rays actually meet (can be shown on a screen); a virtual image is where they only appear to come from
  • a converging lens used as a magnifying glass (object closer than F) gives a virtual, upright, magnified image
  • Supp. a diverging lens spreads rays out; long sight is corrected with converging lenses, short sight with diverging lenses
3.2.5

Dispersion

  • a prism splits white light into the spectrum: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet
  • violet light is refracted most and red least, because each colour has a different speed in glass — they have different frequencies
  • light of a single frequency is monochromatic Supp.

3.3Waves, light & sound

One family of waves, from radio to gamma

Radio, microwaves, infrared, light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are all electromagnetic waves: the same speed, different wavelengths — and very different uses and dangers.

1

Understand

Two sections and a checklist

3.3.1

The electromagnetic spectrum

  • in order of decreasing wavelength (increasing frequency): radio, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays
  • all are transverse and travel at the same speed in a vacuum: 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately the same in air)
3.3.2

Uses and dangers

Microwavessatellite TV, mobile phones, microwave ovensinternal heating of body cells

Supp. Communication uses microwaves for satellites (they pass through the atmosphere) and mobile phones; optical fibres carry infrared or visible light for broadband.

3.4Waves, light & sound

Sound is a vibration you can hear

A vibrating object pushes the air into compressions and rarefactions. Their frequency sets the pitch, their amplitude the loudness.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

3.4.1

How sound travels

  • sound is produced by vibrating sources and is a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions
  • it needs a medium: it cannot travel through a vacuum
  • speed in air ≈ 330–350 m/s; sound travels faster in liquids and fastest in solids
  • human hearing: about 20 Hz to 20 kHz
3.4.2

Pitch, loudness and echoes

  • pitch depends on frequency; loudness on amplitude
  • an echo is reflected sound; distance to the reflector = speed × time ÷ 2
  • measuring the speed of sound: stand a measured distance from a wall, clap and time the echo (or time between seeing and hearing a distant clap)
3.4.3

Ultrasound Supp.

  • ultrasound has a frequency above 20 kHz
  • uses: medical scanning of soft tissue (e.g. a fetus), non-destructive testing of materials for cracks, sonar for measuring the depth of the sea
