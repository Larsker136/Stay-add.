7.1Human nutrition

A balanced diet

Your body needs seven kinds of nutrient, in the right amounts for your age, sex and how active you are. Too little of one and deficiency diseases follow.

1

Understand

Four sections

7.1.1

What a balanced diet is

A balanced diet contains all the nutrients in the correct proportions, and the right amount of energy. What is “right” depends on:

  • age — growing children and teenagers need more protein and energy for their size
  • sex — males usually need more energy, having more muscle
  • activity — an active person needs more energy than someone who sits all day
  • pregnancy and breastfeeding — extra energy, protein, iron and calcium for the baby

7.2–7.3Human nutrition

A tube nine metres long

Food goes in at the mouth and what cannot be used leaves at the anus. In between it is chewed, churned, digested and absorbed — five stages, in order.

1

Understand

Five sections

7.2.1

Five processes

Ingestion
taking substances such as food and drink into the body through the mouth
Digestion
breaking down food into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed — physical (mechanical) and chemical (by enzymes)
Absorption
movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood
Assimilation
uptake and use of nutrients by cells
Egestion
passing out of food that has not been digested or absorbed, as faeces, through the anus

7.3.1

Physical digestion

Definition Physical digestion is the breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules.

  • it gives a larger surface area for enzymes to act on, so chemical digestion is faster
  • done by teeth chewing, the stomach churning, and bile emulsifying fats into small droplets Supp.
  • peristalsis — waves of muscle contraction — pushes food along the canal
7.3.2

Teeth

ToothShapeJob
Incisorschisel-shaped, sharp edgebiting and cutting
Caninespointedgripping and tearing
Premolars, molarsbroad, with ridges (cusps)chewing and grinding
  • a tooth has enamel (hardest substance in the body) over dentine, around a pulp cavity with nerves and blood vessels; the root sits in the jaw bone, held by cement
  • tooth decay: bacteria in plaque feed on sugars and make acid, which dissolves enamel and dentine
  • prevent it by eating less sugar, brushing with fluoride toothpaste, flossing and visiting the dentist
7.3.3

Summary and key terms

  • ingestion → digestion → absorption → assimilation → egestion
  • mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → anus; pancreas, liver and gall bladder add juices
  • teeth, churning and bile break food into smaller pieces — larger surface area
Peristalsis

7.4Human nutrition

Enzymes cut big molecules small

Starch, proteins and fats are too big to cross the gut wall. Three groups of enzymes break them into molecules small and soluble enough to absorb.

1

Understand

Four sections

7.4.1

Why chemical digestion?

Definition Chemical digestion is the breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules.

Only small, soluble molecules can pass through the wall of the small intestine into the blood. Enzymes speed up the breakdown at body temperature.

7.4.3

Hydrochloric acid and bile

  • the stomach makes hydrochloric acid, which kills pathogens in food and gives the acid pH (about 2) that pepsin works best at Supp.
  • bile is made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder, and is alkaline: it neutralises the acid from the stomach, giving the slightly alkaline pH that enzymes in the small intestine need Supp.
  • bile also emulsifies fats — breaks them into tiny droplets, increasing the surface area for lipase (physical, not chemical) Supp.
7.4.4

Summary and key terms

  • amylase: starch → maltose; maltase: maltose → glucose (Supp.)
  • protease: protein → amino acids; lipase: fats → fatty acids + glycerol
  • HCl kills pathogens and suits pepsin; bile neutralises and emulsifies (Supp.)
Enzyme
a protein that acts as a biological catalyst
Pepsin
a protease made in the stomach, working best in acid (Supp.)
Bile
an alkaline liquid made by the liver that neutralises acid and emulsifies fats

7.5Human nutrition

Villi: a tennis court of surface

The small intestine is folded into millions of finger-like villi, each covered in microvilli. That huge surface, one cell thick and full of blood, absorbs digested food fast.

1

Understand

Three sections

7.5.1

Where absorption happens

  • most digested food and water is absorbed in the small intestine, mainly the ileum
  • the colon absorbs most of the remaining water
  • small molecules cross the wall by diffusion and active transport
7.5.2

How a villus is adapted Supp.

FeatureHow it helps
millions of villi, with microvilli on their cellsa very large surface area
wall one cell thick (epithelium)short distance for diffusion
dense network of capillariesblood carries glucose and amino acids away — keeps the gradient steep; they go to the liver in the hepatic portal vein
lacteal (lymph vessel) in the middleabsorbs fatty acids and glycerol
cells with many mitochondriaenergy for active transport of glucose and amino acids
7.5.3

Summary and key terms

  • small intestine absorbs digested food and most water; colon absorbs the rest of the water
  • villi: large surface area, thin wall, capillaries, lacteal (Supp.)
Villus (villi)
a finger-like projection of the small intestine wall
Microvilli
tiny folds of the cell membrane on the villus epithelium (Supp.)
Lacteal
a lymph vessel in a villus that absorbs fats (Supp.)
