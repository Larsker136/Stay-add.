4Biological molecules

Big molecules from small pieces

Starch, fats and proteins are large molecules built from smaller ones joined in chains. Digestion breaks them back down; cells build them up again.

Large molecules and their building blocks

Glucose is a simple sugar; maltose and sucrose are made from two sugar units. Sugars are soluble; starch, cellulose and glycogen are not, which makes them good for storage.

Five tests, five colour changes

Each nutrient has a reagent that changes colour when it is present. Learn the reagent, the method and both colours — before and after.

1

Three sections

4.4

The food tests

Test forMethodNegativePositive
Starchadd a few drops of iodine solutionorange-brownblue-black
Reducing sugars (e.g. glucose)add Benedict’s solution; heat in a water bath at about 80 °C for a few minutesbluegreen → yellow → orange → brick-red (more sugar, further along)
Proteinadd biuret solution (or sodium hydroxide then copper sulfate)bluepurple / lilac
Fats and oilsethanol emulsion test: shake with ethanol, then pour into waterstays clearcloudy white emulsion
Vitamin Cadd the food solution drop by drop to DCPIPstays blueblue colour disappears (decolourised)

Sucrose is not a reducing sugar, so it gives a negative Benedict’s test.

4.5

Doing them safely and fairly

  • solid food is first ground up with a little water to make a solution or suspension
  • wear eye protection; heat Benedict’s tubes in a water bath, not a flame
  • ethanol is flammable — keep it away from flames
  • run a control with water instead of food to see the negative colour
  • Benedict’s is semi-quantitative: compare colours, or the fewer drops of food needed to decolourise DCPIP, the more vitamin C
4.6

Summary and key terms

  • iodine: starch → blue-black · Benedict’s + heat: reducing sugar → brick-red · biuret: protein → purple
  • ethanol: fat → cloudy emulsion · DCPIP: vitamin C → decolourised
Reagent
a chemical used in a test, which changes colour in the presence of a substance
Emulsion
tiny droplets of one liquid spread through another

4Biological molecules

The double helix, and the water around it

DNA is two strands held together by pairs of bases that always match. And everything in a cell happens dissolved in water.

1

Understand

Three sections

4.7

The structure of DNA Supp.

  • DNA is made of two strands coiled together into a double helix
  • each strand carries a sequence of chemicals called bases; there are four: A, T, C, G
  • bases on opposite strands pair up and hold the strands together with bonds: A always pairs with T, and C always pairs with G
  • so if one strand reads ATGC, the other must read TACG

A – T · C – G

4.8

Water as a solvent Supp.

  • many substances dissolve in water, and most reactions in cells take place in solution in the cytoplasm
  • digestion: food molecules must be dissolved to be absorbed into the blood
  • transport: blood plasma carries dissolved glucose, ions, urea and hormones; xylem carries dissolved mineral ions; phloem carries dissolved sucrose
  • excretion: urea and excess salts leave the body dissolved in urine
4.9

Summary and key terms

  • DNA: two strands, double helix, bases paired A–T and C–G (Supp.)
  • water dissolves substances for reactions, transport, digestion and excretion (Supp.)
Base
one of the four chemicals A, T, C, G whose sequence carries genetic information
Complementary base pairing
A always bonding with T, and C with G
Solvent
a liquid in which substances dissolve
