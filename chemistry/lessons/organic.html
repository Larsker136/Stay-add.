11.1Organic chemistry

Formulae, functional groups and homologous series

Carbon atoms link into chains, and a small group of atoms on the chain decides how the whole molecule reacts.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

11.1.1

Formulae

  • molecular formula: the number and type of each atom, e.g. C₂H₆O
  • structural formula: shows how atoms are arranged without drawing every bond, e.g. CH₃CH₂OH
  • displayed formula: shows every atom and every bond
  • Supp. structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different structures, e.g. butane and methylpropane
11.1.2

Homologous series

  • a homologous series is a family of compounds with the same general formula and the same functional group, and so similar chemical properties
  • each member differs from the next by CH₂; physical properties (such as boiling point) show a trend
  • the functional group is the atom or group of atoms that decides the chemical properties
  • saturated: only single C–C bonds; unsaturated: at least one C=C double bond
11.1.3

Naming

SeriesFunctional groupEndingFirst members
alkanes—‑anemethane, ethane, propane, butane
alkenesC=C‑eneethene, propene, butene
alcohols–OH‑olmethanol, ethanol, propanol, butanol
carboxylic acids–COOH‑oic acidmethanoic, ethanoic, propanoic, butanoic acid

The first part of the name gives the number of carbon atoms: meth‑ 1, eth‑ 2, prop‑ 3, but‑ 4. For a full step-by-step method, use the Naming organic compounds lesson.

11.2Organic chemistry

Fuels, alkanes, alkenes, alcohols and acids

Crude oil gives us fuels and the raw materials for plastics. Changing one functional group turns a fuel into a drink, an acid or a perfume.

1

Understand

Five sections and a checklist

11.2.1

Fuels

  • coal, natural gas and petroleum are fossil fuels; methane is the main part of natural gas
  • petroleum (crude oil) is a mixture of hydrocarbons, separated by fractional distillation into fractions: refinery gas, gasoline/petrol, naphtha, kerosene/paraffin, diesel oil/gas oil, fuel oil, lubricating oil, bitumen
  • down the column: longer chains, higher boiling points, more viscous, less volatile
11.2.2

Alkanes

  • saturated hydrocarbons, general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂; generally unreactive except for combustion
  • complete combustion → carbon dioxide + water; incomplete combustion → carbon monoxide and soot
  • Supp. substitution with chlorine in ultraviolet light: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (photochemical)
11.2.3

Alkenes

  • unsaturated hydrocarbons with a C=C double bond, general formula CₙH₂ₙ
  • made by cracking larger alkanes with high temperature and a catalyst; cracking makes alkenes and hydrogen and meets the demand for petrol
  • test: bromine water turns from orange to colourless with an alkene, stays orange with an alkane
  • Supp. addition reactions: with bromine, with hydrogen (nickel catalyst), with steam (phosphoric acid catalyst) to make ethanol
11.2.4

Alcohols

  • ethanol is made by fermentation (glucose + yeast, 25–35 °C, no oxygen: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂) or by the catalytic addition of steam to ethene
  • Supp. fermentation: renewable but slow and batch, less pure; from ethene: fast, continuous and pure but uses non-renewable crude oil
  • uses of ethanol: solvent and fuel; combustion: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O
11.2.5

Carboxylic acids

  • ethanoic acid is a weak acid: reacts with metals, bases and carbonates to make salts (ethanoates)
  • Supp. made by oxidising ethanol (with acidified potassium manganate(VII) or by bacterial oxidation)
  • Supp. carboxylic acid + alcohol → ester + water (acid catalyst), e.g. ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate; esters smell fruity

11.3Organic chemistry

Polymers and plastics

Join thousands of small molecules end to end and you get a polymer: a plastic bag, a nylon rope — or a protein in your body.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

11.3.1

Polymers

  • polymers are large molecules built up from many small units called monomers
  • plastics are made from polymers; different polymers have different units and/or linkages
  • addition polymers form from alkene monomers, with no other product: ethene → poly(ethene)
  • Supp. draw the repeat unit of an addition polymer from its monomer and work out the monomer from the polymer
11.3.2

Condensation polymers Supp.

  • two different monomers each with two functional groups join, and a small molecule (water) is eliminated at each link
  • nylon (polyamide): a dicarboxylic acid + a diamine — amide links
  • PET (polyester): a dicarboxylic acid + a diol — ester links; PET can be converted back to monomers and re-polymerised
  • proteins are natural polyamides made from amino acid monomers
11.3.3

Plastics and the environment

  • most plastics are non-biodegradable
  • disposal problems: they fill landfill sites, harm marine life in the oceans, and burning them releases toxic gases
  • reduce, reuse and recycle; some can be converted back into monomers
