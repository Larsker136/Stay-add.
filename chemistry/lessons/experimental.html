12.1Experimental techniques & chemical analysis

Measuring and titrating

Good chemistry starts with the right apparatus and an accurate reading — then a titration can tell you a concentration to two decimal places.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

12.1.1

Apparatus for measurement

QuantityApparatusUnit
timestopwatchs
temperaturethermometer°C
massbalanceg
volumeburette, volumetric pipette, measuring cylinder, gas syringecm³
  • a volumetric pipette measures one fixed volume very accurately; a burette delivers a variable volume accurately; a measuring cylinder is less accurate
12.1.2

Solutions

  • solvent: the substance that dissolves a solute; solute: the substance that dissolves; solution: the mixture of the two
  • saturated solution: one containing the maximum amount of solute at that temperature
  • residue: what stays on the filter paper; filtrate: the liquid that passes through
12.1.3

Acid–base titrations

  • fill the burette with one solution; use a pipette to put a known volume of the other into a conical flask with a few drops of indicator
  • add from the burette, swirling, until the indicator just changes colour — the end point
  • a rough titration first, then accurate repeats until two results are within 0.10 cm³ (concordant); take the mean
  • suitable indicators: methyl orange or thymolphthalein (universal indicator changes gradually, so it is not used)

12.2Experimental techniques & chemical analysis

Separating and purifying

Mixtures can be pulled apart because their parts differ: one dissolves and one doesn’t, one boils first, one travels further up the paper.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

12.2.1

Paper chromatography

  • used to separate and identify mixtures of soluble substances (such as dyes)
  • draw the base line in pencil; keep the spots above the solvent; a pure substance gives a single spot
  • Rf = distance moved by the substance ÷ distance moved by the solvent front
  • Supp. colourless substances are made visible with a locating agent; identify substances by comparing Rf values with known samples
12.2.2

Separation techniques

TechniqueSeparatesExample
filtrationinsoluble solid from a liquidsand from water
crystallisationsoluble solid from its solutioncopper(II) sulfate crystals
simple distillationa solvent from a solutionpure water from sea water
fractional distillationmiscible liquids with different boiling pointsethanol from water; crude oil
chromatographysoluble substances, e.g. dyesfood colourings
12.2.3

Purity

  • a pure substance has a sharp, fixed melting point and boiling point
  • impurities lower the melting point (and widen its range) and raise the boiling point
  • purity matters in food and drugs, where impurities could be harmful
