5.1Chemical energetics

Energy in, energy out

Every reaction moves energy between the chemicals and their surroundings. Which way it goes depends on the bonds broken and the bonds made.

1

Understand

Three sections and a checklist

5.1.1

Exothermic and endothermic

  • an exothermic reaction transfers thermal energy to the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings increases (e.g. combustion, neutralisation, many oxidation reactions)
  • an endothermic reaction takes in thermal energy from the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings decreases (e.g. thermal decomposition, photosynthesis, dissolving ammonium nitrate)
  • the enthalpy change ΔH is the transfer of thermal energy during a reaction: negative for exothermic, positive for endothermic
5.1.2

Reaction pathway diagrams

  • a reaction pathway (energy level) diagram shows the energy of the reactants and products and the hump between them
  • exothermic: products lower than reactants; endothermic: products higher
  • the activation energy Eₐ is the minimum energy that colliding particles must have to react — from the reactants up to the top of the hump
  • Supp. label the diagram with ΔH and Eₐ using arrows; a catalyst lowers Eₐ but not ΔH
5.1.3

Bond breaking and bond making Supp.

  • breaking bonds is endothermic (energy in); making bonds is exothermic (energy out)
  • ΔH = energy to break the bonds in the reactants − energy released making the bonds in the products
  • if more energy is released making bonds than is needed to break them, the reaction is exothermic

ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds made)
