10.1Chemistry of the environment

Water and fertilisers

Clean water and well-fed crops both depend on chemistry — and both can go wrong when substances end up where they shouldn’t.

1

Understand

Four sections and a checklist

10.1.1

Testing water

  • anhydrous copper(II) sulfate turns from white to blue, and anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride from blue to pink, when water is present
  • pure water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C
  • distilled water is used in practical chemistry because it contains fewer chemical impurities than tap water
10.1.2

Substances in natural water

  • beneficial: dissolved oxygen (for aquatic life), metal compounds that provide essential minerals
  • harmful: some metal compounds (toxic), plastics (harm aquatic life), sewage (microbes), nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers and detergents (eutrophication)
10.1.3

Water treatment

  • sedimentation and filtration remove solids
  • carbon removes tastes and odours
  • chlorination kills microbes
10.1.4

Fertilisers

  • ammonium salts and nitrates are used as fertilisers
  • NPK fertilisers provide nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium for improved plant growth
  • excess fertiliser washed into water causes eutrophication: algal bloom, decomposition by bacteria, loss of dissolved oxygen, death of aquatic life

10.2Chemistry of the environment

Air quality and climate

Clean air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. Burning fuels adds gases that harm health, damage buildings and warm the planet.

1

10.2.3

The greenhouse effect and climate change

  • the Earth’s surface absorbs sunlight and emits infrared; greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄) absorb and re-emit infrared, so less escapes to space
  • increasing greenhouse gases cause global warming, which leads to climate change
  • reduce CO₂ and CH₄: plant trees, use renewable energy and hydrogen fuel, reduce livestock farming, decrease waste in landfill
10.2.4

Reducing pollution

  • catalytic converters in cars convert CO, oxides of nitrogen and unburned fuel into CO₂, N₂ and H₂O
  • Supp. e.g. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂
  • using low-sulfur fuels and flue gas desulfurisation (calcium oxide) reduces sulfur dioxide
  • photosynthesis removes CO₂: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (using light energy and chlorophyll)
