1.1States of matter

Solids, liquids and gases

Everything is made of particles that never stop moving. How close they are and how fast they move decides whether a substance is a solid, a liquid or a gas.

Understand

Four sections and a checklist

1.1.1

The three states

SolidLiquidGas
Arrangementregular, close togetherrandom, close togetherrandom, far apart
Movementvibrate about fixed positionsmove around each othermove quickly in all directions
Shapefixedtakes the shape of the containerfills the container
Volumefixedfixednot fixed — easily compressed
Forces between particlesstrongweakervery weak

This is the kinetic particle theory: the particles always move, and they move faster when the temperature is higher.

1.1.2

Changes of state

  • melting (solid → liquid) at the melting point; freezing is the reverse at the same temperature
  • boiling (liquid → gas) happens throughout the liquid at the boiling point; condensation is the reverse
  • evaporation is liquid → gas at the surface only, at any temperature below the boiling point; it is faster when warmer, with a bigger surface area or with air moving over the surface
  • while a substance melts or boils its temperature stays constant: the energy taken in is used to overcome the forces between particles, not to raise the temperature — the flat parts of a heating curve
  • a pure substance melts and boils at fixed temperatures; impurities lower the melting point and raise the boiling point

When heating, particles gain kinetic energy; when cooling, they lose it and the curve runs the other way, with flat parts at the boiling and melting points.

1.1.3

Gases: temperature and pressure

  • gas pressure comes from particles colliding with the walls of their container
  • raising the temperature: particles move faster and collide harder and more often → at constant pressure the gas expands (its volume increases)
  • raising the pressure pushes the particles closer together → the volume decreases
  • a gas is easy to compress because there is lots of space between the particles
1.1.4

Diffusion

  • diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, caused by their random movement
  • it happens in gases and liquids (slowly in liquids, because the particles are closer and move more slowly) — e.g. the smell of perfume spreading, purple potassium manganate(VII) spreading through water
  • Supp. the lower the relative molecular mass of a gas, the faster it diffuses at the same temperature
  • Supp. ammonia (Mr 17) and hydrogen chloride (Mr 36.5) released at opposite ends of a tube make a white ring of ammonium chloride closer to the hydrogen chloride end
