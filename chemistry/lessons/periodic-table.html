8.1The Periodic Table

Order in the Periodic Table

Arrange the elements by proton number and patterns jump out: columns that behave alike, and rows that run from metals to non-metals.

1

Understand

Two sections and a checklist

8.1.1

Groups and periods

  • elements are arranged in order of increasing proton number
  • a vertical column is a group: elements in a group have the same number of outer-shell electrons and similar chemical properties
  • a horizontal row is a period: elements in a period have the same number of occupied shells
  • for groups I–VII, the group number = the number of outer electrons; Group VIII (0) has full outer shells
8.1.2

Trends

  • across a period the elements change from metals (left) to non-metals (right)
  • metals are on the left and in the middle; the dividing line runs in a staircase from boron to astatine
  • the charge of an element’s ion is linked to its group: I → 1+, II → 2+, III → 3+, V → 3−, VI → 2−, VII → 1−
  • the table can be used to predict the properties of elements from the trends in their group

8.2The Periodic Table

Alkali metals, halogens, transition elements and noble gases

Four families, four personalities: soft metals that fizz in water, coloured non-metals that swap places, hard coloured catalysts, and gases that do nothing at all.

1

Understand

Four sections and a checklist

8.2.1

Group I: the alkali metals

  • lithium, sodium, potassium: soft (cut with a knife), low density, low melting points, shiny when freshly cut
  • react with cold water: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen, e.g. 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂; the solution is alkaline
  • down the group: reactivity increases, melting point decreases, density increases
  • use the trend to predict rubidium and caesium: even more reactive, even lower melting points
8.2.2

Group VII: the halogens

HalogenFormulaColourState at r.t.p.
chlorineCl₂pale yellow-greengas
bromineBr₂red-brownliquid
iodineI₂grey-blacksolid
  • halogens are diatomic non-metals; down the group colour darkens, density increases and reactivity decreases
  • a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from a solution of its halide: Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂
  • Supp. displacement is redox: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂
8.2.3

Transition elements

  • found in the central block (e.g. iron, copper, chromium, nickel)
  • high densities and high melting points; form coloured compounds; the elements and their compounds often act as catalysts (iron in the Haber process)
  • Supp. they can form ions with variable oxidation numbers, e.g. Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺
8.2.4

Noble gases

  • Group VIII (0): helium, neon, argon …
  • monatomic and unreactive because their outer shells are full
  • uses: helium in balloons (low density, unreactive), argon in light bulbs (unreactive)
